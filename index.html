Rakaat

How many, and what changes

A rakaat is one full cycle: stand, bow, prostrate twice, and you're back. Prayers are just that cycle repeated a set number of times. Almost every beginner's confusion is about what changes between one cycle and the next — so here it is, spelled out.

PrayerRakaatVoiceWhat's different
The three rules that explain everything 1. Al-Fatihah is recited in every rakaat — in the Shafi'i school this holds even when you are following an imam. 2. The extra short surah is added only in rakaat 1 and 2 — never in the 3rd or 4th. 3. You sit for a middle tashahhud after rakaat 2 whenever the prayer has more than two rakaat. Learn those three and you can work out any prayer yourself.
Three things that only apply behind an imam The walkthroughs below assume you are praying alone, which is where you'll start. In congregation: you always recite silently, even in the prayers marked aloud — the "aloud" applies to the imam. You don't add a short surah in the loud prayers; you listen to his instead. And if you arrive late and catch the imam already bowing, that rakaat still counts for you and you are not required to complete Al-Fatihah for it.
Two exceptions to the counts above On Friday, Jumat prayer replaces Dzuhur and is 2 rakaat rather than 4, preceded by the khutbah. And when travelling, the 4-rakaat prayers may be shortened to 2 (qasr). Neither is something to worry about yet — but the table isn't the whole picture, and it's better to know that now than to be confused in a mosque later.

What has been checked, and what hasn't. Every Quranic verse in this app — all of Al-Fatihah, the four short surahs, and the anchor verses in the stories — has been compared word by word against the Uthmani text from Quran.com, and matches. The recitations of the prayer itself are a different matter: the iftitah, tashahhud and salawat come from hadith rather than the Quran, they have several valid narrations, and they have not been verified here against a primary source. Read those alongside Step By Step Salah or with your teacher before memorising, and if their wording differs from the wording here, use theirs.

Where schools differ, it says so. The fiqh here follows the Shafi'i school. Points where practice itself is split — qunut in Subuh, saying the intention aloud — are marked rather than settled, because both positions have established backing and your mosque will have its own custom.
