Rakaat

How many, and what changes

A rakaat is one full cycle: stand, bow, prostrate twice, and you're back. Prayers are just that cycle repeated a set number of times. Almost every beginner's confusion is about what changes between one cycle and the next — so here it is, spelled out.

The three rules that explain everything 1. Al-Fatihah is recited in every rakaat. 2. The extra short surah is added only in rakaat 1 and 2 — never in the 3rd or 4th. 3. You sit for a middle tashahhud after rakaat 2 whenever the prayer has more than two rakaat. Learn those three and you can work out any prayer yourself.
Before your first mosque visit (behind an imam)
The walkthroughs below assume you are praying alone, which is where you'll start. In congregation you always recite silently, even in the prayers marked aloud — the "aloud" applies to the imam. Whether you still recite Al-Fatihah yourself is one of the points the schools differ on; see "Where the schools differ" in the In the prayer section. And if you arrive late and catch the imam already bowing, that rakaat still counts for you.
Fridays and travelling
On Friday, Jumat prayer replaces Dzuhur and is 2 rakaat rather than 4, preceded by the khutbah. And when travelling, the 4-rakaat prayers may be shortened to 2 (qasr). Neither is something to worry about yet — but the table isn't the whole picture, and it's better to know that now than to be confused in a mosque later.

Settings

Settings

Language and school are separate preferences. Changing one never resets the other.

Keeping Faham on your phone — open the address in Chrome or Safari, then use the browser menu: “Add to Home screen” on Android, Share → “Add to Home Screen” on iPhone. After the first visit it works with no connection.

What has been checked, and what hasn't — every Quranic verse in this app has been compared word by word against the Uthmani text from Quran.com, and matches. The recitations of the prayer itself are a different matter: the opening supplication, tashahhud and salawat come from hadith, have several valid narrations, and have not been verified against a primary source here. Read them alongside your teacher before memorising, and if their wording differs, use theirs.

Four schools, one guide — the prayer walkthrough, the Witr guidance, the wudhu list and the Asr convention all follow the school you pick in Settings. Where a school contains more than one respected opinion, the alternative is stated rather than hidden. The jurisprudential content here is still awaiting review by a qualified reader before release.

Privacy — no account, no server, no analytics. Your prayer history and your location never leave this device; the prayer times and the qibla are calculated here.

The English and Indonesian renderings here are written for clarity; they are not copies of any published translation.
